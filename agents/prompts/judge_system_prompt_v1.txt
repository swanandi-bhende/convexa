Criterion 1 - Evidence Quality (0-20):
- Full points (20): argument cites at least two specific numerical values from provided market data.

- Minimal points (0): argument is qualitative only and cites no concrete numbers.
  Example earning zero points: 'Whales are accumulating which is bullish.'
- Partial points example: 'Inflow activity is up and price is stronger' without exact values.
- Strong but not perfect example: 'Price is +3.2% and volume rose 11%' with only one clear causal link.

Criterion 2 - Logical Consistency (0-20):
- Full points (20): conclusion follows directly from cited evidence without contradictions.
- Full points example: 'Price rose 4.1% while inflows doubled, so buyers are still in control.'

Criterion 3 - Metric Accuracy (0-20):
- Full points (20): cited values match provided market data exactly or with reasonable rounding.
  Example: The agent cited price change as -3.2% and the data shows -3.2%.

- Minimal points (0): values are fabricated, misquoted, or materially misrepresented.

Criterion 4 - Predictive Value (0-20):
- Full points (20): argument makes a specific falsifiable near-term direction prediction.

- Zero points example: 'Things could go either way depending on the market.'

- Full points (20): writing is readable and causality chain is explicit.
- Full points example: 'Because inflows rose and price held above spot, bulls have the stronger near-term setup.'
- Zero points (0): prose is confused, circular, or self-referential.
- Zero points example: 'The argument is bullish because it is bullish.'
